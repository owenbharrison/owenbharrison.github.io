Usage

• Use the W/A/S/D keys to move the camera around.
• Use Shift/Space to move the camera up/down.
• Use the ARROWS to look around.
• Press Enter to toggle the model's spinning.
• Press G to toggle the model movement gizmo. When it is active, use the left cursor to:
 • Drag the arrows to move along an axis,
 • Drag the rectangles to move it along a plane.
• Press R to toggle the camera render view.
• Press F to toggle the camera frustum view.

Overview

This project uses a set of virtual cameras in 3d space to try and triangulate detected movement in a scene.

Each frame, each camera takes a photo. For each camera, the pixel-wise difference is taken between its previous photo, and the current one. These differences are used to calculate a weighted average in the photo's screen space of where the most pixels changed. Then, a ray is projected from camera's position thru that point. (note: if the difference is negligible, no ray is cast.)


Then, the intersection of all said camera rays is found using gradient-descent. This is necessary because tiny amounts of error in the system make the rays skew against one another. The sum of distances between a candidate point and each ray is used as the cost function to minimize. This intersection point should be the point in 3d space where the object was moving! (note: if there are <2 rays, no axes is shown.)

In the program, the camera rays are shown in white, and the intersection point is shown as rgb axes. The purple wireframes around each camera denote its field of view.

For those wondering, the program simulates the calibration of each camera to calculate its focal length during program startup. I did this because in the real world, if you were to set up such a system using cheap cameras, their focal length is rarely known, and hence must be measured.

Possible Use Case

Imagine you are a farmer, and pesky crows keep attacking your crop. You could place several cheap cameras around your plot, then measure their relative positions and orientations. Then with this system you could monitor the crow positions and make the scarecrow move to scare them away.

Imagine you are a movie director, and your motion capture software stops working. You could place several cheap cameras around your studio, then measure their relative positions and orientations. Then with this system you could monitor the actor positions and map a cgi skeleton to their poses.
